Getting Around Whistler by Transit

  • FREE public transit throughout Whistler Valley
  • Convenient service connecting villages, neighborhoods, and day lots
  • Year-round service with extended hours during peak seasons
  • Accessible buses with bike racks and ski storage

Whistler Transit System

Whistler's public transit system provides free transportation throughout the valley, making it easy and convenient to get around without a car. The fleet of modern, accessible buses connects all major areas including Whistler Village, Creekside, Function Junction, Emerald Estates, and surrounding neighborhoods.

During winter, buses are equipped with ski and snowboard racks. In summer, bike racks are available on all buses, making it easy to explore Whistler's extensive trail network.

Main Routes

Route 1 - Village

  • Connects Whistler Village to Creekside
  • Stops at major hotels and accommodations
  • Frequent service every 10-20 minutes
  • Operates from early morning to late evening

Route 2 - Neighborhoods

  • Services residential areas
  • Connects to day parking lots
  • Service every 20-30 minutes
  • Links Emerald, Alpine, and Nordic areas

Route 3 - Valley Express

  • Rapid service along main corridor
  • Links Function Junction to Village
  • Limited stops for faster travel
  • Peak hours service

Service Hours

Transit service operates year-round with extended hours during peak winter and summer seasons.

Operating Hours

Winter Season (December - April)

  • Weekdays: 6:00 AM - 2:00 AM
  • Weekends: 6:00 AM - 3:00 AM
  • Holidays: Extended late-night service
  • Increased frequency during peak ski hours

Summer Season (May - November)

  • Daily: 6:00 AM - 12:00 AM
  • Reduced frequency in shoulder seasons
  • Special event service for festivals
  • Bike rack availability on all buses

Regional Connections

BC Transit also operates regional routes connecting Whistler to surrounding communities:

  • Route 99: Connects Whistler to Pemberton (45 minutes)
  • Squamish Connector: Limited service to Squamish (1 hour)
  • Vancouver Airport: Private coach services available (multiple departures daily)

Tips for Using Transit

  • Plan ahead: Check real-time schedules and route maps at bus stops or online
  • Signal the driver: Wave at approaching buses to indicate you want to board
  • Ski/bike racks: Load your own equipment; drivers will assist if needed
  • Pets: Small pets in carriers are welcome; service animals always permitted
  • Exact change: While service is free in Whistler, regional routes may require payment
  • Download the app: Real-time tracking available for all routes

Accessibility

All Whistler transit buses are wheelchair accessible with low-floor entry and designated seating areas. Priority seating is available near the front of each bus. Service animals are always welcome, and drivers are trained to provide assistance to passengers with disabilities.

Why Choose Transit?

Using public transit in Whistler offers many benefits:

  • Completely free within Whistler Valley
  • Avoid parking hassles and fees
  • No need to worry about après-ski drinks and driving
  • Environmentally friendly alternative to driving
  • Relax and enjoy the scenery
  • Convenient connections to all major areas
